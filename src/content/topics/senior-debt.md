Senior debt describes a position in the financing structure. Priority matters because multiple providers may have claims on the same borrower or business value. The meaning of senior depends on the actual agreements, relevant entities and security arrangements. It does not simply mean the loan with the largest balance, earliest date or lowest interest rate.

The collection gives a concrete closing example: a subordination deed places bank lending ahead of seller financing. That is evidence of relative ranking between those creditors, not a complete account of all enforcement rights or payment restrictions. A full understanding would require the agreed documents and the borrower and guarantor structure. [Source · E221](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=byJSV1CEYBk)

For a buyer, priority has consequences beyond a label in the capital stack. Identify which entities owe the debt, what assets support it, which payments require consent and whether other group businesses guarantee repayment. The source's steel-group experience shows how cross-guarantees can spread trouble between companies. The covenant discussions also show that lender control can restrict acquisitions and distributions before a payment default occurs. A senior lender's protection may improve its willingness to lend while reducing the sponsor's flexibility. Evaluate those consequences together instead of assuming that priority only becomes relevant after a business fails.
