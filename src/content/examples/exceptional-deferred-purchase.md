## The reported transaction

Sey describes a historical acquisition with roughly £950k adjusted sustainable EBITDA, rounded to £1m in conversation. The disclosed total price is £1.8m: £1m at completion, £400k at month 24 and £400k at month 36. He presents it as an unusually favorable deal enabled by the seller family's circumstances. The status is reported completed; the source account is not independent verification of the transaction or subsequent performance. [Watch source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EL60pBzPd_k)

## Reconcile the price

| Payment point | Amount | Cumulative consideration |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Completion | £1,000,000 | £1,000,000 |
| Month 24 | £400,000 | £1,400,000 |
| Month 36 | £400,000 | £1,800,000 |

The full price is therefore £1m + £0.4m + £0.4m = £1.8m. Two valuation calculations explain the rounding:

- £1.8m ÷ £1m rounded earnings = **1.8 times**.
- £1.8m ÷ £0.95m adjusted earnings = **about 1.89 times**.

The second ratio is an editorial calculation using the more precise stated earnings estimate. It is not a different price or a correction to the seller-payment schedule. Completion represents about 55.56% of the total, leaving 44.44% for the two later payments.

## Interpret carefully

The schedule preserves early cash relative to paying the entire price immediately, but creates two concentrated later needs. The collection does not provide a full bank facility, tax model, working-capital bridge or evidence of every payment's eventual discharge. A favorable multiple alone cannot establish the return realized by shareholders.

The most important limit is explicit in the speaker's description: this was exceptional. It is useful for learning how earnings precision and payment timing affect interpretation, not for promising that buyers routinely obtain profitable businesses on these terms.
