## Fictional Acquisition Companion example

This hypothetical uses invented facts and numbers. A fictional distributor reports £420,000 of EBITDA. During diligence, the seller requests an adjustment for £120,000 of marketing costs. The buyer's sample records support £36,000 as a one-time launch campaign; the remaining £84,000 relates to activity expected to continue.

## Reconcile the proposed adjustment

1. Reported EBITDA: **£420,000**.
2. Portion supported as a one-time cost in this case: **£36,000**.
3. Ongoing marketing expense retained: £120,000 − £36,000 = **£84,000**.
4. Candidate adjusted EBITDA: £420,000 + £36,000 = **£456,000**.

The arithmetic does not establish that £36,000 qualifies. The buyer still needs source documents, confirmation that the expense is included in reported earnings, and evidence that removing it will not require replacement spending.

## Keep the operating need in the forecast

An expense can be unusual and still be necessary to maintain customers or replace a one-off activity. Test the commercial purpose, timing and effect of removal, then model any replacement cost. Apply a valuation method only after the earnings base is supported. Continue with [adjusted EBITDA](/topics/adjusted-ebitda/) and [due diligence](/topics/due-diligence/).
