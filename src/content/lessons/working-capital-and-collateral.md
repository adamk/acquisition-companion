## What it means
Working capital is the operating funding tied to the timing of customer receipts, stock and supplier payments. In this collection, its acquisition significance is straightforward: the company still needs resources to trade after the seller has been paid. Sey's discussion of cash-free, debt-free terms distinguishes excess cash from the working capital needed to keep operating, although his broader wording about removing liabilities is simplified. [Watch source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b_Emk0J2yfw)

Collateral is a separate issue: which assets a lender will accept as backing for a facility. An asset can be necessary for operations, already pledged or unsuitable for a proposed lender. Reading a balance-sheet total alone will not answer those questions. [Watch source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b_Emk0J2yfw) [Watch source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=18cB9LOaucs)

## Why it matters
Counting the same resources twice creates a funding plan that only works on paper. For example, cash required for payroll cannot simultaneously be treated as freely removable seller proceeds in your operating model. Similarly, an invoice book supporting an existing financing arrangement should not be assumed to provide an entirely new acquisition facility. The source raises exactly that second concern when interpreting short-term creditors. [Watch source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b_Emk0J2yfw) [Watch source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b_Emk0J2yfw)

The reverse can also be surprising. Quick customer payment can be commercially attractive while leaving little receivables collateral. Sey's Irish service-business discussions illustrate why good collection and easy acquisition financing are not identical attributes. [Watch source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=akgHprIWcZU)

## How it works
Read the notes behind each balance-sheet line. Identify what the debtor balance contains, how old those balances are and whether amounts arise from genuine customer invoices or other relationships. In one source case, roughly €952,000 of total debtors includes about €600,000 of trade debtors; the balance includes amounts associated with group undertakings. Sey treats those categories differently when considering borrowing capacity. [Watch source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=18cB9LOaucs)

Then investigate existing facilities. Find out whether receivables financing is already in use and what must happen to that facility at completion. The source's inference from accounts is a useful question to ask, not confirmation of an actual encumbrance without the underlying documents. [Watch source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b_Emk0J2yfw)

## Practical interpretation
Build two schedules: an operating-cash schedule and a possible collateral schedule. The first asks how the company pays wages and suppliers while waiting for customer receipts. The second asks which assets might support a lender and on what basis. This editorial exercise separates the two purposes that the balance-sheet discussions repeatedly connect. [Watch source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b_Emk0J2yfw) [Watch source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=18cB9LOaucs)

For each proposed funding asset, record your uncertainty rather than applying an automatic percentage. A receivable awaiting acceptance of project work may have a different risk profile from an accepted invoice for delivered goods. Sey's differing advance-rate illustrations reflect that dispute risk; they are not quotes available to every buyer. [Watch source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WcYMjlTsUwA)

## A worked example
**Source case with an editorial calculation.** Begin with the Irish accounts' approximately €952,000 debtor total. If only €600,000 represents trade debtors, the remaining €352,000 cannot simply be added to a trade-receivables borrowing calculation. Even the €600,000 requires further assessment of eligibility, age, disputes and existing financing. The subtraction clarifies categories; it does not estimate an approved loan. [Watch source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=18cB9LOaucs)

The accounts discussed were not a current lender borrowing-base certificate. Do not convert the speaker's interpretation into a legal conclusion that intercompany balances have no recoverable value. The narrower conclusion is that total accounting debtors and eligible lending collateral are different measures.

## Common mistakes
Avoid assuming cash-free, debt-free means the business has no continuing operating liabilities. Avoid presenting a seller's bank balance as surplus without examining timing. And avoid treating a general statement about receivable age limits as a universal lender condition: the source gives product generalizations without full facility terms.
