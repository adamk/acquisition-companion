## What it means
Asset-based finance begins with assets that a lender is prepared to fund against. The collection focuses on plant, machinery and trade receivables. Sey contrasts this with lending based primarily on cash generation. His simple secured-versus-unsecured explanation is not a complete product taxonomy; the useful distinction is which evidence the proposed lender needs to assess capacity. [Watch source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1C5gI6LlWhA)

An asset's accounting value is not necessarily its lending value. In the source's machinery illustration, an independent appraisal asks what equipment could realize under specified sale conditions before an advance percentage is applied. [Watch source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Hvw5FAbRAZY)

## Why it matters
Multiplying book assets by a convenient percentage can overstate the available funding. The machinery example contains two separate reductions: from book value to appraised realization value, then from appraised value to the amount advanced. Missing the first step makes the acquisition look better funded than the example supports. [Watch source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Hvw5FAbRAZY)

Receivables involve a related distinction. A billed amount may be disputed, retained or already used in another financing arrangement. Sey's comparison between accepted deliveries and construction work highlights why the legal and commercial quality of an invoice matters alongside its face value. [Watch source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WcYMjlTsUwA) [Watch source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b_Emk0J2yfw)

## How it works
For machinery, identify the assets and the appraisal basis. The source describes a 120-day sale assumption with the buyer collecting equipment from the site. Those assumptions belong to that illustration; they are not universal lending requirements. A different valuation basis could produce a different result before anyone discusses a facility percentage. [Watch source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Hvw5FAbRAZY)

For invoices, start with trade receivables rather than total debtors. Then ask which invoices are eligible and whether there are disputes, retention amounts or existing claims. Sey gives rough advance-rate comparisons to explain risk differences. Treat them as teaching examples, not current lender offers. [Watch source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=18cB9LOaucs) [Watch source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WcYMjlTsUwA)

## Practical interpretation
Make a funding worksheet with columns for accounting amount, eligible amount, valuation basis, assumed advance and existing debt. Mark every unconfirmed input. This is an editorial tool derived from the source's appraisal and debtor-note examples. Its purpose is to prevent a precise-looking borrowing total from concealing uncertain inputs. [Watch source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Hvw5FAbRAZY) [Watch source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=18cB9LOaucs)

Keep operational consequences visible. A property sale-and-leaseback may release money while introducing rent into the operating model. Sey discusses that route as an alternative to retaining property, but supplies no full transaction terms. The funding benefit and the continuing operating cost must be considered together. [Watch source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wd4Y23wruXU)

## A worked example
**Source hypothetical.** Machinery has a £1m book value. Under the example's sale assumptions, an appraiser values it at £800,000. Applying an illustrative 80% advance produces £640,000 of possible funding. Applying 80% directly to book value would instead produce £800,000, overstating this example by £160,000. These are illustrative inputs, not a facility quotation. [Watch source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Hvw5FAbRAZY)

This calculation still does not establish net acquisition proceeds. Existing asset finance, fees or lender conditions would need to be resolved for an actual transaction. The source separately warns that assets already supporting borrowing cannot simply be assumed to support a fresh, unencumbered loan. [Watch source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b_Emk0J2yfw)

## Common mistakes
Avoid combining asset-based capacity and cash-flow capacity without confirming how a lender treats the same company and collateral. Avoid assuming every invoice is equivalent to cash. Finally, do not infer that a lender is a nonbank merely because it offers cash-flow or asset-based lending: lending method and institution type are different classifications in the collection. [Watch source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Hvw5FAbRAZY)
