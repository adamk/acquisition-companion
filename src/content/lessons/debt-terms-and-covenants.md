## What it means
Debt terms explain much more than the interest rate. They specify repayment, maturity and conditions that govern the borrower's actions. In Sey's accounts, financial tests and restrictions on acquisitions, control changes or distributions can materially affect how a company is run. The source does not provide a complete loan agreement, but it shows why these provisions deserve attention before completion. [E159: original discussion](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rE2R3B0kiBc)

Amortization is the scheduled repayment of principal. A shorter repayment period can create heavy cash demands even where the business remains profitable. The source's experience of roughly £75,000 monthly payments illustrates this pressure without providing a complete interest-versus-principal breakdown. [E007: original discussion](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9OBSog0S5XU)

## Why it matters
A covenant can interfere with an acquisition strategy even when the underlying business is trading acceptably. Sey reports accepting a restriction on another HVAC acquisition outside a lender's security group, then facing a repayment demand after a separate purchase. That self-reported case shows a mismatch between financing permissions and the intended growth plan. [E006: original discussion](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9OBSog0S5XU)

Security arrangements can also spread risk across companies. His account of steel-group cross-guarantees describes failures in two businesses threatening others. Separate legal entities are not enough to establish isolation when contractual obligations connect them. [E091: original discussion](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=np9jH5OyQnE)

## How it works
Read the cash schedule and the permissions together. When are interest and principal due? What transactions require consent? Which financial measures are tested, when and using whose definitions? Sey discusses cash-balance timing around payroll and collections, illustrating why a test date matters as well as the numerical threshold. [E231: original discussion](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=je7CsLaUm8w)

Then map ranking and exposure. The completion material describes a subordination deed placing the bank ahead of sellers who provide seller finance. It establishes senior and junior positions in that transaction, but not the full payment blocks, enforcement rights or exceptions. Those missing details cannot be supplied by the label subordinated alone. [E221: original discussion](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=byJSV1CEYBk)

## Practical interpretation
Create a one-page operating calendar showing payments, reporting dates and actions needing consent. Add a diagram of guarantors and secured entities. This editorial review tool translates the source's covenant and group-guarantee experiences into questions an owner can monitor. It is not a substitute for the agreement or its professional interpretation. [E159: original discussion](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rE2R3B0kiBc) [E091: original discussion](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=np9jH5OyQnE)

Compare alternative facilities on usable cash and operating flexibility, not only quoted rate. Keep personal guarantees explicit. Sey's own position changes: he reports signing a guarantee despite earlier opposition and later refinancing out of it. That is a transaction-specific experience, not proof that guarantees are always necessary or always avoidable. [E213: original discussion](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KsnY8Gpnkl4)

## A worked example
**Hypothetical cash-pressure exercise based on reported payments.** A business must pay £75,000 each month under a facility. Three months of those payments total £225,000 regardless of whether customer collections arrive evenly. This simple calculation explains why a profitable year can still contain difficult months. The reported source does not disclose enough to reconstruct the complete facility. [E007: original discussion](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9OBSog0S5XU)

For covenant interpretation, use a separate source illustration: a £1m monthly revenue budget with an allowed 20% shortfall implies an £800,000 threshold under those assumed terms. It is an example, not a covenant recommendation or evidence of an executed facility. [E159: original discussion](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=je7CsLaUm8w)

## Common mistakes
Do not use the source's rough debt-service coverage ratio as a universal bank definition. His earnings proxy omits adjustments that may matter to an actual lender. Do not assume a desire for covenant-light borrowing proves such financing was obtained. And do not read future refinancing as an unconditional release from today's obligations.
