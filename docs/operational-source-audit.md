## Source gaps deliberately left open

**P1 — integration execution.** Detailed coverage is thin on biased personnel evaluations, knowledge transfer, competing cultures, redundancy timing and systems cutover. Seek attributable practitioner process accounts with limitations, plus empirical employee/customer evidence with methodology and time period. Do not fill this gap with generic culture essays.

**P1 — synergy realization.** The corpus does not establish realization percentages, overrun frequencies, expected delay or a market discount rate. Future retrospective sources should distinguish announced savings, achieved recurring savings, cash implementation costs and observed revenue losses.

**P1 — employment execution.** Consultation, redundancy, retention agreements and successor obligations need authoritative jurisdiction-specific sources and professional review. Do not improvise those requirements.

No new P0 attribution or firsthand-experience claim was introduced. These gaps limit authoritative instruction; conditional reasoning and labeled fictional exercises remain useful. No HP–Compaq or other historical merger facts were added.

## Original fictional exercise

**Two companies, one management team** is an advanced exercise in Deal Lab and IC Challenge. Four stages cover stabilization, people/evidence, planned economics and mediocre execution. One operations head has stronger scheduling evidence; the other owns major customer relationships. Neither has led at combined scale. Payroll/billing knowledge, sales handovers and uncertain intentions separate temporary retention from long-term leadership. A manager's ranking does not establish an employee's motive.

Code calculates only the incremental, pre-tax integration project versus staying separate, not acquisition value or a probability-weighted outcome.

- Combined baseline EBITDA: $1m. Proposed recurring benefit: $200k/year, **20% of that EBITDA denominator**.
- At-close costs: systems $120k + severance $60k + retention $40k + training $30k = $250k.
- Conditional plan: year-end net cash flows $40k, $200k, $200k, $200k, $200k.
- Mediocre execution: half the savings, one further year of delay, doubled at-close costs, duplicate-running costs in years 1–2, and 5% revenue loss from year 2 at 30% contribution margin. Net flows: -$60k, -$210k, -$50k, -$50k, -$50k.
- Assumed five-year horizon, 10% discount rate, no terminal value: project NPV **+$362,703** versus **-$830,862**. These are exercise assumptions, not market standards or empirical forecasts.

Distraction, departures and working-capital disruption remain unquantified, not zero, and are not silently added a second time to modeled customer loss.
